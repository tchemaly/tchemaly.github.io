About

I work at the intersection of augmented reality, computer vision, and surgery — building systems that let surgeons see through microscopes, past soft tissue, and into pre-operative imaging in real time. Most of my work lives between the OR and the headset: hand tracking, stereo calibration, and mixed-reality overlays for procedures like temporal bone surgery and microvascular reconstruction.

Lately I've been focused on the perceptual side of the problem — how people actually process what a headset shows them, and where that breaks down in ways plain accuracy metrics miss, like cybersickness and geometry mismatch. I'm a PhD candidate at Stanford, and always interested in collaborations at the intersection of XR, HCI, and medicine.
